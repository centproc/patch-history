<h3>Core Gameplay</h3>
<p>
<i>If this is your first time hearing about 2XKO, avert your eyes! This section is super in-depth, and you should just log in and try the game out. If you have tried the game before and you're curious about what we've done since you last played, read on.</i>

We've been making a lot of changes to the game based on your feedback. In Alpha Lab 2, we noticed matches ran longer than desired based on a few different factors, including the strength of defensive options, an abundance of Break meter, high combo starter proration, and an ineffective comeback mechanic.

After Alpha Lab 2, it became clear that we needed to focus on four key areas to bring the gameplay experience to a level that we were happy with. Here they are:

* <h3>Match Pacing</h3>
    - To improve match pacing, we made changes to starting Break meter, starting Super meter, Super damage, and combo starter proration, effectively upping damage across the board.
* <h3>Offensive vs. Defensive Options</h3>
    - In Alpha Lab 2, some defensive options didn't have direct counterplay, and others let the defender get away very easily. In light of that, we took a look at wake up rolls, push assist, retreating guard, and dashing.
* <h3>Comeback Potential</h3>
    - The above changes caused higher lethality overall, so comeback potential is more important than ever. We adjusted Fury and Break meter generation to make sure matches are still anybody's game until the very end.
* <h3>Match Engagement</h3>
    - During Alpha Lab 2, we noticed there were certain points in a match where players wouldn't interact. Whether they were waiting for one of their champions to heal, or trying to build Break or Super meter, this pattern caused a ton of time outs. To encourage getting into the fight, we changed gray health generation and Super meter rules.

We think we've made strong strides in creating a more cohesive, engaging, and compelling system, but we know we won't get everything right immediately. We'll continue to monitor how balanced our offensive and defensive mechanics feel, movement effectiveness, comeback potential, and the power levels of Fuses.

You might notice even more changes to core gameplay when you're in-game, but the section below covers a lot of them!

<h3>Super Meter</h3>

* Super meter resets between rounds. By default, teams start each round with one bar of Super meter.
    * If a team is using Juggernaut or Sidekick Fuse, they'll start each round with two bars of Super meter.
* After a successful parry, damage scaling on the next hit landed is now slightly reduced. (It'll deal a bit more damage than it did before.)
* Low parry no longer applies damage scaling to the next hit landed.

<h3>Break Meter</h3>

* Break meter now starts at 75%.
* Break meter bonus while getting hit by long combos has been increased.
* Break meter bonus no longer increases during Handshake Tags or cinematics.
* On champion KO, no longer receive bonus 50% Break meter.

<h3>Defense Tools</h3>

* Backdashing, jump blocking, Retreating Guard, and pushblock are all slightly less powerful.
    * Pushblock now has a longer cooldown, and the pushback distance has been decreased.
    * Retreating Guard travel distance has been decreased.
* All attacks from Assist champions are now mid attacks. (i.e. Assists can never be low or overhead.)
* Counter hits now grant meter, a damage boost, and additive hit stun.
* Counterhit <strong>M</strong> and <strong>H</strong> attacks now grant an untechable state against airborne opponents.
* Counterhit <strong>L</strong> attacks now grant additional air hit stun.
* Fury Break has been buffed. Duration, chip damage from normal and special attacks, ground speed, and defense boosts have all been increased.
* Crossup protection has been reduced from three frames to one frame.
* Getup attack is now easier to punish from air block.

<h3>Additional Changes</h3>

* Gray health (champion health that can regenerate while tagged out) recovers more slowly.
* Attacks generate less gray health overall.
* Super damage and minimum scaling (the floor for how much an attack can deal after being reduced by damage scaling) has been increased.
* <strong>L</strong> and <strong>M</strong> attack proration (damage reduction on hitting the same move repeatedly within one combo) has been reduced.
* When Assist champions take damage, 75% is dealt as gray health. The rest is unrecoverable. (Previously, 100% of damage to Assist champions was dealt as gray health.)
* Throws deal more damage, have a longer recovery time, and cause a counter hit state on whiff.
* Throwable volumes are more consistent.
* Throws now have new visual effects to show the tech window more clearly.
* Forward and air throws now cause hard knockdown. Grounded recovery rolls can now be thrown.
* Limit Strike no longer causes hard knockdown.
* Air throw landing recovery has been reduced.
* Air throws are now consistent across champions and hit slightly behind.
* You can no longer Handshake Tag when a throw doesn't connect with an opponent.
* Chain-dashing is easier.
* Backdash travel distances have been reduced for all champions.
* Jump startup has been slowed from three to four frames
* Timing restriction on Double Down has been removed. (You can now enter Double Down at any time during the point champion's Super.)
* Juggernaut Fuse has been buffed.
    * While using Juggernaut, gain more Break meter on a clean hit. Pushblock now costs Break meter instead of Super meter, and pushblocking pushes both champions.
    * On a clean Eject hit, the Juggernaut player will recover a small amount of gray health. The Ejected opponent will lose a large amount of their gray health.

<h3>Quality of Life</h3>

* When landing an attack, pressing ( <strong>L</strong> + <strong>M</strong> Dash, <strong>L</strong> + <strong>H</strong> parry, etc.) will now properly result in a Parry or Dash, rather than another attack.
* You can now enter Training mode directly from any online lobby by opening the menu. This also works when in a duo. Doing this will remove you from the lobby you were in.
* When queue times are long, our matchmaking system will look for opponents from other lobbies. If you find a match this way, you'll see a hologram of your opponent's avatar in your lobby.
* You can now enter the Casual or Ranked lobby directly from Training mode by opening the menu. This also works when in a duo.
    * <i>Known bug: if you enter Training mode from the main menu and choose "Go to Casual Lobby" or "Go to Ranked Lobby" from the Training mode menu, you'll return to the main menu. These options only work if you use them in a Casual or Ranked lobby.</i>
* The controls menu has been completely revamped. Remapping controls should take less time across any compatible peripheral.
* In-game player reporting is now enabled. It can be accessed from the Avatar Interaction menu in lobbies, Match History, the Social panel, and chat messages.
* In-game player blocking is now enabled. It can be accessed from the Avatar Interaction menu and Match History.
</p>

<h3>Champion Adjustments</h3>
<p>
We wanted to increase champion power levels to ensure that offense stays strong and neutral remains interesting. Generally, champions can now perform stronger mixups and have more interesting decisions to make while on offense and in neutral.

The changes listed below are not exhaustive. There are adjustments to hurtboxes, hitboxes, meter, and minimum scaling or damage to encourage more interesting routing. We’ll be going over those in detail in future patch notes, but you all forgot how much damage Braum’s mid-air <strong>S2</strong> did anyway. (Right?)
</p>

<h2 id="braum">Braum</h2>

<br>

<p>
Braum was in a pretty good spot, but reversal command grab (<i>Fight, Mighty One</i> ↓ <strong>S1</strong>) was clearly going to be a problem. We slowed command grab significantly and reduced his health. In return, his overhead is much faster and his normals are a bit better.

* Health has been slightly reduced.
* <strong>M</strong> and ↓ <strong>M</strong> attack recovery has been reduced.
    * Braum should be strongest with a proactive teammate, but his neutral was a bit too risky to survive on his own.
* <i>Concussive Blow</i> (↘ <strong>M</strong>) startup has been reduced.
* <i>Fight, Mighty One</i> (↓ <strong>S1</strong>) startup has been significantly increased.
    * Unbreakable version now causes crumple.

</p>

<strong><a href="/patch-history/docs/2xko/2025-09-16-2xko-patchnotes-cb#top" style="text-decoration: none;">return to top</a></strong>

<h2 id="darius">Darius</h2>

<br>

<p>
We gave Darius a projectile to give him more interesting ways to play neutral and pressure the opponent. As the rest of the roster developed around Darius, we felt those aspects of his game were a bit flat.

* Health has been slightly reduced.
* <i>Cut Down</i> (↘ <strong>M</strong>) has a new animation that's harder to read. (It's not actually faster. Let the reaction wars begin.)
* <i>Cut Down</i> (↘ <strong>M</strong>) is cancelable into supers
* <i>Grievous Wheel</i> (previously ↓ <strong>S2</strong> ) has been moved to ↘ <strong>S2</strong> and now has a shockwave projectile.
    * This makes it harder to safely mash Darius's reversal on defense, while making room for his new projectile.
* When playing on the Noxus stage, additional soldier NPCs will spawn in the background.
</p>

<h2 id="ekko">Ekko</h2>

<br>

<p>
The change to forward throw and air throw helped Ekko's knockdown game a lot, but the difference between when Ekko scores a hard knockdown versus when Ekko makes the opponent block was too high. <i>Tangled Timeline</i> (<strong>S2</strong> Super) helps a lot with soft knockdown, but his on-block pressure wasn't strong enough. All of Ekko's mix options are now significantly stronger against a blocking opponent.

* <i>Mid-air</i> [<strong>H</strong>] now pulls in grounded opponents on block.
* <i>Flip It</i> (↘ <strong>M</strong>) startup has been reduced.
* <i>Attempt #37</i> (<strong>S1</strong>+<strong>S2</strong>) has a brand new cinematic
* <i>Charged Chronostrike</i> (→[<strong>S2</strong>]) now passes through opponents on block, and is now -1 on block.
    * We wanted to give Ekko more sauce, and decided that enabling this was both really fun and had adequate counterplay with Retreating Guard, team pushblock, and reversal throw.
* <i>Chronobreak</i> ([<strong>S2</strong>]<strong>S2</strong>) is now safe on block.
* <i>Phase Dive</i> (↓<strong>S2</strong>/↓[<strong>S2</strong>]) now passes through opponents in the corner.
* <i>Timewinder</i> no longer hits knocked-down opponents.
    * Previously, <i>Timewinder</i> hitting knocked-down opponents made Ekko's more advanced combos drop based on slight timing differences, and this move OTGing was never a good situation for Ekko.
    * This change makes his combos more stable and leads to fewer frustrating drops.
</p>

<h2 id="illaoi">Illaoi</h2>

<br>

<p>
Illaoi's gameplay was a little linear. She would swing with <strong>M</strong>, ↓ <strong>M</strong>, and <i>Riptide</i> (→ <strong>S1</strong>), which was a bit too similar to Darius. To get her tentacles more involved, we took a bit of power away from <strong>M</strong> and → <strong>S1</strong>, and invested it in making ↓ <strong>S2</strong> chargeable, which gives Illaoi a full-screen option so she's always a threat.

* Health has been slightly reduced.
* <strong>M</strong>, ↓ <strong>M</strong>, and <i>Riptide</i> (→ <strong>S1</strong>) have been nerfed by reducing distance traveled and increasing the amount of time that hurtboxes linger.
    * Illaoi shouldn't rely on these moves so zealously.
* <i>Harsh Lesson</i> (→ <strong>S2</strong>) startup has been reduced.
* <i>From the Depths</i> (↓ <strong>S2</strong>) can now be charged to have Nagakabouros strike from below at any distance, which tracks the opponent's position and summons a tentacle on contact.
    * This change keeps opponents on their toes and gives Illaoi more access to tentacles, so she's less feast-or-famine.
</p>

<h2 id="jinx">Jinx</h2>

<br>

<p>
Jinx's zoning tools were largely overshadowed by the strength of <i>Fishbones!</i> (<strong>S2</strong>) in the air. Rather than nerf <i>Fishbones!</i>—remember, we're trying to make champs stronger—we've made improvements to <i>Fly Clappy, Fly!</i> (↓ <strong>S1</strong>), <i>Flame Chompers!</i> (→ <strong>S1</strong>), and charged <i>Fishbones!</i>(<strong>S2</strong>).

* <i>Watch Your Head!</i> (↘ <strong>H</strong>) is slightly faster, and is now cancelable into specials on hit.
* <i>Fly Clappy, Fly!</i> (↓ <strong>S1</strong>) and Flame Chompers! (→ <strong>S1</strong>) have less recovery.
    * <i>Clappy</i> (monkey bomb) and <i>Chompers</i> (bomb with teeth…?) were powerful, but it was hard to justify using them over using jump-back Fishbones! These changes make <i>Clappy</i> and <i>Chomper</i> more useful in neutral and open up more combo applications.
* <i>Clappy</i> will no longer trigger on knocked down opponents, and if it happens to occur it will OTG.
    * We want <i>Clappy</i> to help your combos, not hurt them!
* <i>Flame Chompers!</i> (→ <strong>S1</strong>) cannot hit until landing and activating. The tossed version took up too much space for the impact it has on contact, especially in her Assist.
* <i>Flame Chompers Assist</i> (<strong>T</strong> or → <strong>T</strong>) now uses the "kick" version of <i>Chompers</i> as opposed to the "toss" version.
    * This increases Jinx's corner pressure and mixup potential, and being able to juggle <i>Chompers</i> midscreen allows for more fun teamplay interactions.
* You can now hold → or ← during <i>Fishbones, Hop!</i> (↓ <strong>S2</strong>, any variation) to adjust the launch arc.
* <i>Zaaaapp!</i> (<strong>S2</strong> Super) now hits OTG.
</p>

<h2 id="yasuo">Yasuo</h2>

<br>

<p>
We're happy with Yasuo's gameplan, but he was a bit too honest when making the opponent block. Yasuo's mixup is much better now, particularly when solo, and Turbulent Wind has new visual effects and behavior. Face the wind.

* <i>Sweeping Blade</i> (→[<strong>S1</strong>]) charge startup has been reduced, and it now steals corner.
* <i>Recovery of Calm</i> (↓ <strong>S1</strong>) movement followups—<i>Zephyr Step</i> (↓ <strong>S1</strong> > <strong>S1</strong>) and <i>Updraft</i> (↓ <strong>S1</strong> > <strong>S2</strong>)—has been reduced.
* <i>Turbulent Wind</i> (<strong>S2</strong>) projectile has received visual and behavior cleanups to address weird edge cases.
* <i>Windwall</i> and <i>Turbulent Wind</i> (← <strong>S2</strong>, <strong>S2</strong>) whirlwind now hits OTG. Cool new routes!
* Launcher adjustments.↓ <strong>H</strong> forward movement range has been decreased, and its recovery has been increased.
    * This was a very strong button which sometimes failed in a primary intended use case (anti-air); both should be more in line now.
</p>

<h2 id="vi">Vi</h2>

<br>

<p>
People loved Vi at Evo, and we agree! We left her core kit alone, and tuned damage (and <i>Blast Shield's</i> comboability) to put her more in-line with the rest of the champions. Don't worry, she still hits like a truck.

* Vi's damage at Evo was pretty wild. We've normalized her damage, particularly around normals, <i>Footwork</i> (→ <strong>S1</strong>) suite, <i>Crater Maker</i> (↓ <strong>S2</strong>), and <i>Crater Maker Assist</i> (←  <strong>T</strong>).
* <i>Blast Shield</i> (↓ <strong>S1</strong>) leading to a combo was problematic. The intended followup is now <i>Relentless Force</i> (<strong>S1</strong> Super).
* <i>Footwork > Hook</i> (→ <strong>S1</strong> > L) was too strong in neutral with too little recovery. It now has more recovery.
    * We want players to dodge and weave, but <i>Hook</i> was too abuseable.
* <i>Footwork > Knuckle Down</i> (→ <strong>S1</strong> > <strong>H</strong> ) can now be properly anti-aired with .
</p>
